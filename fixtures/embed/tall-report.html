Checkout latency review

Payments, week 41. The p99 of the checkout call rose from 420 ms to 2.4 s for 38 minutes.

Summary

A connection pool limit on the payments gateway was reached during the 14:00 batch. Requests queued behind the batch and timed out at the edge. The fix raised the pool and split the batch.

Read the full payments runbook, the appendix beside this file, or this script link, which never runs.

Timeline

14:00 The nightly settlement batch starts and opens 180 connections.

14:04 Checkout p99 crosses 1 s. The on-call engineer is paged.

14:17 The pool limit is identified from the gateway dashboard.

14:31 The batch is paused. Latency recovers within two minutes.

14:38 The pool is raised from 200 to 400 and the batch resumes in two halves.

15:10 The incident is closed. No payment was lost; 1,204 checkouts were retried by clients.

Impact

For 38 minutes, roughly one checkout in six took longer than two seconds. Mobile clients retried automatically. Web clients showed a spinner, and support received 41 tickets.

Why it happened

The settlement batch and live checkout share one connection pool. The batch was sized when it opened 60 connections; it has grown with the merchant count and now opens three times as many.

Nothing alerted on pool saturation. The first signal was latency at the edge, eleven minutes after the batch started.

The gateway configuration, before and after

Before: one pool of 200 connections for all callers, no per-caller limit.

After: a pool of 400, with the batch capped at 120 and checkout guaranteed 200.

The batch now runs in two halves, 20 minutes apart, so its peak is half what it was.

A saturation alert fires at 80% of the pool for two minutes.

Costs

Support time for 41 tickets, about six engineer hours of incident work, and an estimated 0.4% of the hour's checkouts abandoned. No refunds were issued.

The larger pool costs nothing extra on the current gateway plan.

Follow-up

Separate pools for the batch and for live traffic, owned by the payments team, due at the end of the month.

A load test that runs the batch and peak checkout together, owned by platform, due in two weeks.
